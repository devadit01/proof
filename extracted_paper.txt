[6] ​ABSTRACT
[7] This research details the formulation design, process optimization, and comprehensive physicochemical characterization of a topical hydrogel system containing a combination of 2.0% w/w Salicylic Acid (keratolytic active) and 10.0% w/w Turmeric Extract (Curcuma longa, anti-inflammatory active)[3,4]. Using Carbopol 940 (0.40% w/w) as a cross-linked gelling matrix, three experimental batches (F1, F2, F3) were formulated by varying co-solvent ratios and neutralizing agent concentrations [1, 2].
[8] The optimized formulation (F3) demonstrated superior physical stability, desirable rheological flow, appropriate spread ability, and a skin-compatible pH value of 5.8 ± 0.2[1,4]. Comparative analysis with a standard commercial 2% salicylic acid gel revealed superior humectants properties and a non-greasy, smooth skin feel [1, 3]. The findings indicate that the developed hydrogel offers a balanced, synergistic approach for managing inflammatory dermatological conditions [3, 4].
[9] ​	Keywords:-  Salicylic Acid, Turmeric Extract, Curcuma longa, Hydrogel Optimization, Carbopol 940, Topical Delivery Systems, Physicochemical Evaluation.
[10] ​
[11] 1. INTRODUCTION
[12] ​	Topical dermatological formulations rely heavily on the balance between active therapeutic agents and vehicle compatibility [1, 4]. Among inflammatory skin disorders, acne vulgaris and hyper pigmentation require dual-action therapies that combine exfoliative and anti-inflammatory mechanisms. [3,4]
[13] ​
[15] Actives Profile
[16] ​	Salicylic Acid (2.0% w/w): A lipophilic beta-hydroxy acid (BHA) that penetrates deeply into the pilosebaceous unit[3,4].It works by dissolving intercellular cement, shedding dead epidermal cells, and clearing comedones[1,3].
[17] ​	Turmeric Extract / Curcuma longa (10.0% w/w): Rich in bioactive polyphenols known as curcuminoids (curcumin, desmethoxycurcumin, bisdemethoxycurcumin) [3]. It exhibits potent free-radical scavenging, anti-inflammatory (via NF-κB pathway suppression), and antimicrobial activities against skin pathogens such as Cutibacterium acnes [3, 4].
[18] ​2. RATIONALE OF THE STUDY
[19] ​	While 2% salicylic acid is a standard monotherapy for topical exfoliation, its use is frequently accompanied by localized adverse effects, including dry skin, scaling, erythema, and barrier disruption [3, 4].
[20] Integrating a high concentration of Turmeric Extract (10%) serves a dual purpose:
[21] ​Synergistic Efficacy: Combines chemical exfoliation with anti-inflammatory soothing [3].
[22] ​Side-Effect Mitigation: Offsets salicylic acid-induced irritation via natural antioxidants and skin-soothing bioflavonoid [3, 4].
[23] ​The target formulation utilizes an aqueous hydrogel structure to ensure a light, non-occlusive, non-greasy skin feel suitable for acne-prone skin types [1, 4].
[24] ​3. EXPERIMENTAL WORK
[25] ​Role of Ingredients and Excipient Profile
[26] Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs):
[27] ​		Salicylic Acid (2.0% w/w) — Keratolytic agent [1, 3].
[28] ​		Turmeric Extract (10.0% w/w) — Herbal active/antioxidant [3].
[29] ​Co-solvents & Solubilizers:
[30] ​Propylene Glycol (15.0% w/w) — Primary solvent required to completely dissolve crystalline salicylic acid and prevent precipitation upon gelation [1, 2].
[31] ​Methyl Propylene Glycol / Methyl propanediol (1.0% w/w) — Secondary solvent and penetration enhancer; aids in clear dispersion of herbal extracts [1, 2].
[32] ​Humectants & Plasticizers:
[33] ​Glycerine (3.0% w/w) — Retains skin surface moisture and counteracts localized desquamation [2, 4].
[34] ​	Sorbitol (1.0% w/w) — Prevents skin dryness and improves gel texture [2, 4].
[35] ​Gelling Matrix:
[36] ​Carbopol 940 (0.40% w/w) — High molecular weight polymer of acrylic acid cross-linked with allyl ethers of pentaerythritol; provides structural viscosity and clarity [1, 2].
[37] ​Neutralizing Agent / Buffer:
[38] ​Triethanolamine (TEA) (q.s.) — Tertiary amine used to adjust pH, neutralize carboxyl groups on the Carbopol backbone, and trigger gel network expansion[1,2].
[39] ​Vehicle:
[40] ​	Purified Water (q.s. to 100.0%) — Continuous aqueous phase [1, 2].

[45] 3.3 Method of Preparation and Optimization
[46] [Phase A: Hydration]
[47] Carbopol 940 + Warm Purified Water ➔ Hydrate for 12 hours (Swollen Polymer Dispersion)
[48] │
[49] ▼
[50] [Phase B: Active Solubilization]
[51] Salicylic Acid + Propylene Glycol + Methyl Propylene Glycol ➔ Stir until clear solution
[52] │
[53] ▼
[54] [Phase C: Herbal Phase]
[55] Phase B + Turmeric Extract + Glycerine + Sorbitol ➔ Homogenize solution
[56] │
[57] ▼
[58] [Phase D: Blending & Neutralization]
[59] Combine Phase A, Phase B& Phase C ➔ Add TEA dropwise under continuous slow agitation ➔ Gel Formation
[61] PROCEDURE: -
[62] ​Phase A (Polymer Dispersion): Carbopol 940 was sprinkled uniformly over warm Purified Water and allowed to swell overnight to ensure complete hydration without clump formation [1, 2].
[63] ​Phase B (Active Solubilization): Salicylic Acid was dissolved in a blend of Propylene Glycol and Methyl Propylene Glycol under mild agitation until a clear, particle-free solution was obtained[1,2].
[64] ​Phase C (Botanical Blend): Turmeric Extract, Glycerin, and Sorbitol were incorporated into
[65] Phase B and stirred until homogeneous. [2,3].
[66] ​Phase D (Gel Cross-linking): Phase C was slowly poured into Phase A with constant, low-shear mechanical stirring to avoid air entrapment [1].
[67] ​Neutralization: Triethanolamine (TEA) was added dropwise to induce polymer neutralization, converting the liquid phase into a coherent, viscous hydrogel.[1,2].
[68] ​Optimization Strategy (3 Batches)
[69] ​Batch F1 (Low Polymer - 0.3%): Yielded an under-cross linked fluid system with low viscosity and high runnability [1].
[70] ​Batch F2 (High Polymer - 0.5% + High TEA): Resulted in an excessively rigid gel network with high alkaline pH (> 7.2), which compromises salicylic acid bioavailability and causes skin irritation[1,4].
[71] ​Batch F3 (Optimized Formulation - 0.4% Carbopol + Controlled TEA): Achieved an ideal yield value, optimal spread ability, smooth texture, and skin-compatible pH (5.8). [1,4].
[72] ​4. EVALUATION AND ANALYSIS OF FORMULATION
[73] ​The optimized Batch F3 was evaluated across physical, chemical, and functional metrics alongside a standard commercial 2% salicylic acid gel (Control)[1,4].
[74] ​Evaluation Protocol & Results Matrix
[81] 5. INTERPRETATION OF RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
[82] ​Solubilization Stability: Salicylic acid was successfully solubilized within the co-solvent blend (Propylene Glycol + Methyl Propylene Glycol)[1,2].No crystalline precipitation was observed under polarized microscopy, confirming that the co-solvent system prevents active crystallization[1].
[83] ​Rheology & Spread ability: Batch F3 demonstrated pseudo plastic (shear-thinning) flow behaviour [1].High viscosity at rest (21,400 cP) prevents product leakage from packaging, while low viscosity under shear forces ensures smooth application on affected skin areas)[1,4].
[84] ​pH Compatibility: The final pH of 5.80 is compatible with the natural acid mantle of human skin (pH 4.5–5.5)[4].This provides a safe compromise: it maintains salicylic acid efficacy without inducing acid burn or cellular irritation[3,4].
[85] ​Comparative Advantage: Unlike the marketed control product—which showed a post-application drying effect—Batch F3 maintained skin hydration due to the combined action of Glycerin, Sorbitol, and Turmeric Extract [2, 3, 4].
[86] ​6. EXPECTED ADVANTAGES OF DEVELOPED FORMULATION
[87] ​Dual Therapeutic Action: Simultaneous exfoliation (keratolysis) and soothing (anti-inflammatory)[3,4].
[88] ​Enhanced Skin Tolerance: Humectant matrix minimizes localized desquamation, peeling, and dryness commonly caused by standard beta-hydroxy acid gels[2,3].
[89] ​Optimal Aesthetics: Water-based, non-comedogenic, non-greasy hydrogel matrix provides quick absorption and a cooling post-application sensation.[1,4].
[91] ​7. LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY
[92] ​Staining Potential: The high concentration of natural Turmeric Extract (10%) imparts a distinct yellow color that can stain light-colored clothing or bedding if applied heavily. [3, 4].
[93] ​Photo degradation: Curcuminoids are light sensitive [3].The product must be stored in opaque or amber-coloured airless pump containers to prevent photo-oxidation over time.[1,3].
[94] ​Long-Term Stability Testing: Accelerated real-time stability studies (40°C / 75%RH) over 6 months are required to confirm shelf-life shelf behaviour [1].
[95] ​8. CONCLUSION
[96] ​	A polyherbal hydrogel containing 2.0% Salicylic Acid and 10.0% Turmeric Extract was successfully formulated, optimized, and evaluated [1, 3]. Batch F3 achieved optimal physicochemical properties, ideal viscosity, smooth spread ability, and stable pH balance [1, 4].
[97] The inclusion of botanical actives successfully addressed the drying side effects of conventional salicylic acid gels while maintaining structural stability and desirable cosmetic feel [3, 4].
